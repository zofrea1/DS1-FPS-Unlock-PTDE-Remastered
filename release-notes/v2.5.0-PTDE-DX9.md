# Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition, FPS Unlock v2.5.0

Unlocks the frame rate and fixes the physics, camera, timers and HUD that were written for exactly 30 FPS. Files: `xinput1_3.dll`, `PTDE-FPS-Unlock.ini` and `dxvk_d3d9.dll`, next to `DARKSOULS.exe`. Current Steam build only (`SizeOfImage` `0x11C2000`).

Effects that keep their 30 FPS look at any frame rate, a HUD and menus scaled by the game's own placement, no more black specks with the game's anti-aliasing, 10-bit colour by default, and up to 17% more frames at 4K.

Everything new is on by default, and every part has an INI switch.

## Effects

- **Swaying particles** (`FixSfxSway`, new). Hundreds of effects turn their particles by a random angle every few frames (every 5 in most), so at 240 FPS they turned eight times as often as at 30: the Blighttown spider offspring's magma tentacle ends in flames that stay on its body at 30 FPS and trailed far behind it at 240. The particles now count the 30 FPS frames of the game's clock. At 30 FPS nothing changes.
- **Effect trails keep their length** (`FixSfxTrails`, new). The ribbons behind sorceries, projectiles and some enemy attacks hold a fixed number of points, enough for one a frame at 60 FPS, and most took a point every frame, so above 60 FPS they were cut short: to a quarter of their length at 240 FPS (in a test at Firelink Shrine they covered 28% of their lifetime at 240 FPS and 21% at 347). A trail now takes a point at most every 1/60 s, or every 1/30 s where its points would not otherwise cover its lifetime, and keeps its whole length at any frame rate.
- **Particle density corrected** (`FixSfxSpawnRate`). Since 1.3.0 this fix raised short spawn intervals to exactly 1/30 s, which sits on the 30 FPS frame: a frame that came a fraction of a millisecond early spawned nothing, so smoke, sparks and embers came out about a quarter thinner than in the original game, at every frame rate. The intervals are now a hair under 1/30 s. Particles a second at the Demon Ruins bonfire: 877 at 30 FPS, 884 at 60, 862 at 144 and 864 at 240, against the original game's 878 at 30 (and 1,374 at 240 without the fix).

## HUD and menus

- **The HUD scaled by the game's own placement** (`UIScale`, now 50 to 150). Each part of the HUD is a dialog of the game's menu system, and each is now scaled through the game's own transform for it, toward its own corner or edge. Pieces are told apart by the game, not by where they are drawn, so where parts overlap (an enemy's bar over a meter, a menu over the HUD) nothing is pulled out of place any more.
- **Letters with their part** (`UIScaleText`, new, `auto`). In a smaller HUD the text kept the game's letter size: two prompt lines ran into each other, the soul count overfilled its box and a damage number dwarfed its bar. Letters now take their part's size, or a size of their own (50 to 150).
- **The menus scaled too** (`UIScaleMenus`, new, `auto` follows `UIScale`). The start menu and its screens (inventory, equipment, status, system, options), the bonfire's screens (Level Up, Attune Magic, the Bottomless Box, Repair), shops, conversations and message boxes, each toward its own anchor: the screens about the middle, the start menu toward its top right corner, the bonfire's menu and a character's list of choices toward their top left, message boxes toward the bottom edge. A character's lines keep their place and their backdrop; only the letters change size. The mouse follows the menus.
- **Prompts no longer hide a boss's health bar** (`FixBossBarOverlap`, new). The game draws the prompt box (pick up an item, recover lost power) and the item windows over a boss's name and bar, at any HUD size. While a boss bar is shown they now move up above it (above both bars when there are two).

## Picture

- **No black specks with the game's anti-aliasing** (`FixAntialiasingDepth`, new). With the game's anti-aliasing on, grass and leaves showed black specks in a ring around the player (about 13 m away, fainter closer in). The game keeps the scene's depth for the effects in three 8-bit digits, and blending the anti-aliasing's samples at a blade's edge could put that depth metres off, which left specks where the mist and smoke are drawn over it. The game as shipped does it too, on PC and Steam Deck alike. The depth is now kept as one float a sample, in the same memory as the game's, at no measurable cost (Firelink in 4K, 4x anti-aliasing: 14,214 outlying depths without the fix, 158 with it). The same specks were behind the old advice to keep the anti-aliasing off on the Steam Deck. Not beside DSfix.
- **10-bit colour by default** (`HighPrecisionColor = true`). The scene is now kept in 10 bits per colour, in the same memory as the game's 8, with the same smooth fog and dark skies as 16-bit float at about a quarter of float's cost (Firelink at 5760x3240 with 4x anti-aliasing on an RTX 3090: 8-bit 119.5 FPS, 10-bit 115.0, float 102.8). `float` keeps 16-bit float. An INI from 2.3.0 to 2.4.1 that says `true` now gets 10-bit.

## Faster

- **Up to 17% more frames at 4K** with this mod's resolution, with the same picture within frame-to-frame noise. At Firelink in 4K with 4x anti-aliasing (RTX 3090): 263 to 308 FPS. The averaging copies of the scene for the depth of field and the glare, and the depth of field's near blur mask, are made in two passes instead of one long loop per pixel; the game's camera blur, whose weights are 0 while the camera is still, is skipped then; and the anti-aliased depth is no longer resolved a second time when nothing was drawn into it.

## RTX Remix and DSfix

- **RTX Remix**: Options > Brightness works again under `YieldToRtxRemix` (2.4.1 replaced the game's brightness ramp with a flat one), and `DpiAware` stays as the INI says.
- **When `YieldToDSFix` is needed**, said plainly in the README and the INI: only to use DSfix's own picture settings (its resolution, depth of field, HUD scale). To use DSfix 3.0 for its quality-of-life features beside this mod's picture, leave `YieldToDSFix` off and `FixSun` on; the README's [DSfix 3.0](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-FPS-Unlock-PTDE-Remastered#dsfix-30) gives the settings for both ways.

**[Prepare to Co-op Edition](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-Prepare-to-Coop-PTDE) 2.1.0** includes FPS Unlock 2.5.0. If you have it, do not install this as well.

Details for every fix are in the README.

## Install

Copy the three files next to the game exe. Upgrading from 2.4.x: replace `xinput1_3.dll` (`dxvk_d3d9.dll` is unchanged); your INI keeps working, and new settings take their defaults when it does not list them (replace it too for the new notes). Recommended: exclusive fullscreen with `FullscreenRefreshRate = 0`. Back up your saves. Steam Deck and Linux: launch option `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="xinput1_3=n,b" %command%`.

**Online play: no guarantees.** The mod modifies the game in memory; using it on official servers could risk a ban. Use at your own risk; no responsibility is accepted for bans or lost progress (see the README and LICENSE).

## Known limits

Riding the edge of a short step can leave you stuck against it at a high frame rate. In-engine cutscene cameras are blended; a hard cut still snaps, and pre-rendered movies stay 30 FPS. With the HUD scaled above 100, several item windows at once can stack past the top edge. Send `PTDE-FPS-Unlock.log` and any `*-crash.dmp` with bug reports.
