# Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition, FPS Unlock v2.10.0

Lips and ledges climbed as at 30 FPS, no more enemies carried up walls, a cleaner picture in dark places (and 10 bits to HDR displays), and fixes for the Steam Deck. Files: `xinput1_3.dll`, `PTDE-FPS-Unlock.ini` and `dxvk_d3d9.dll`, next to `DARKSOULS.exe`. Current Steam build only (`SizeOfImage` `0x11C2000`).

## Movement

- **Lips a little above a step are climbed as at 30 FPS** (`FixStepUp`, new, on). The game lifts a character by its step height each frame, moves it and puts it back down; a lip a little higher is climbed by creeping up its edge, and the short frames of a high frame rate crept far less. In the Great Hollow's Chloranthy Ring hollow a 0.37 m lip that 30 FPS climbs in a third of a second could not be climbed at 60 to 240 FPS, so the hollow could not be left. A character that is pushing against something but hardly moving is now moved as at 30 FPS: every 1/30 s its frames are moved again as one. That lip now takes 0.27 to 0.38 s at any frame rate from 45 to 240 FPS (0.31 to 0.33 s at 30); what 30 FPS cannot climb stays unclimbable, and movement in the open and along walls is unchanged. Enemies get it too.
- **Enemies no longer carried up walls** (`FixStepDown`). A character pressed against a wall could rise a step at a time: the game's ground check started inside the wall and reported ground above the character, so the frame's step lift was never taken back. At 30 FPS that is a hop of about 1 m; at 120 FPS one of the Lower Undead Burg's door-ambush thieves shot 12 to 16 m into the sky the first time it burst out. Above 30 FPS such a frame now keeps the character at its height: the thief lands and fights as at 30 FPS.

## Picture

- **No more whitish dots in dark places** (`HighPrecisionColor`). The dither from the 10-bit scene into the 8-bit picture was a fixed grid that showed in dark areas as lighter dots crawling with the camera (4.4% of the dark pixels in the Undead Asylum's opening cutscene), more visible in SDR than in HDR. It is now a fine noise, different for red, green and blue and new every frame.
- **10 bits to the screen** (`HighPrecisionOutput`, new, `auto`): with DXVK on a display Windows composes above 8 bits (HDR or Auto Color Management on), and under Proton (the Steam Deck OLED, Linux) in HDR where the game's window takes it, the picture goes to the screen in 10 bits per colour, with no dither at all. Same memory and frame rate.
- **Fixed: too dark with supersampling in exclusive fullscreen** (`Supersampling`, or a `RenderResolution` above the screen). The scale-down left the graphics device's sRGB switches set, and the whole picture could come out much darker. It now does that conversion itself. Windowed and borderless were not affected.

## Steam Deck and DXVK

- **Fixed: grey specks in grass and leaves with anti-aliasing** (`FixAntialiasingDepth`). Under DXVK (the Steam Deck, Linux, `DXVK = true`) specks of the background showed through blade edges while the camera moved: DXVK makes the alpha test at the precision of the target being drawn, so the depth pass and the colour pass disagreed on some edge pixels. The depth pass now makes the colour pass's test. No measurable cost.
- **`AlphaToCoverage` now works on AMD graphics under DXVK** (the Steam Deck), through AMD's own switch; it had no effect there. It costs about 2.5% of the frame time on the Deck at 1280x800 with 4x anti-aliasing.

## Install

Copy the three files next to the game exe. Upgrading from 2.9.0: replace `xinput1_3.dll` (`dxvk_d3d9.dll` is unchanged); your INI keeps working, and new settings take their defaults when it does not list them. Back up your saves. Steam Deck and Linux: launch option `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="xinput1_3=n,b" %command%`.
