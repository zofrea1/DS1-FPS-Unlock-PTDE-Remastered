# Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition, FPS Unlock v2.3.0

Unlocks the frame rate and fixes the physics, camera, timers and HUD that were written for exactly 30 FPS. Files: `xinput1_3.dll`, `PTDE-FPS-Unlock.ini` and `dxvk_d3d9.dll`, next to `DARKSOULS.exe`. Current Steam build only (`SizeOfImage` `0x11C2000`).

## The picture, done anew

2.2.0 made the game faster with the same picture. 2.3.0 makes the picture itself better, with one goal: faithfulness. The game renders at your resolution, and its depth of field, glows, fog, colour and shadows are rebuilt to look the way they were made to look, measured against the original, without the blocks, banding and shimmer a high resolution exposed. DSfix is no longer needed for any of it; it stays fully supported (see [DSfix](#dsfix) below).

Everything new is on by default, and every part has an INI switch.

## Picture

- **Your screen's resolution** (`RenderResolution = auto`). The game drew its world at 1024x720 and its HUD at 1280x720 and stretched both. Now both are drawn at your resolution (or one you pick). Depth of field, glare and bloom keep their original sizes, made from every pixel of the larger scene and read smoothly: they look as the original did, without blocks or shimmer.
- **Depth of field without blocks** (`DepthOfFieldResolution = half`). The blur is made from half-resolution pictures with widths measured to match the game's, about 0.06 ms a frame at 4K. `full` looks almost the same for six times the cost. `DepthOfFieldStrength` (0 to 200%) makes the blur weaker or stronger.
- **Glows, mist, fog and smoke without blocky edges** (`EffectsResolution = half`). Drawn at half your resolution with the game's own depth-aware upsample at the 2x it was made for, the closest to its look. `full` is sharper than the original ever was and heavy with close smoke at 4K.
- **No colour banding** (`HighPrecisionColor`). The scene is kept in 16-bit colour and dithered to your screen, so thin fog, dark skies and the halos of pulsing glows fade smoothly instead of stepping (about 10% of the frame rate).
- **Sharper shadows without the weave** (`ShadowMapResolution = 4096`, `ShadowBias = 0.006`). Lit, rounded surfaces (Quelaag's egg sacs, your own back) no longer show the shadow map's texels as a fine weave, and shadow edges are finer.
- **Anisotropic filtering** (`AnisotropicFiltering = 16`): floors and walls at an angle stay sharp.
- **Smooth foliage edges** with the game's anti-aliasing on (`AlphaToCoverage`): grass, leaves, hair and fences no longer break into dots in the distance. `AntialiasingSamples` (2, 4 or 8) for smoother edges.
- **Supersampling** (`Supersampling`, for example 1.5 or 2): render above your resolution, scaled down as an exact average in linear light, without shimmer.
- **Heat haze rolls again** (`FixSfxDistortion`): above 30 FPS the haze over Quelaag's lava and fire shook instead of rolling.
- **Sun and moon with DSfix's resolution** (`FixSun`): they no longer stay hidden after something passes in front. This mod's own resolution never had the problem.

Drawing at your resolution costs GPU time next to the game's 1024x720. On a weaker PC, set a lower `RenderResolution` (for example `1920x1080`) or turn `HighPrecisionColor` off first; `RenderResolution = off` keeps the game's own picture.

## Screens and HUD

- **Ultrawide screens** (21:9, 32:9) fill the screen like 16:10 ones (`FixAspect`), and the HUD and menus keep their 16:9 shape instead of stretching (`FixAspectUI`). Lock-on and health bars stay over their targets.
- **HUD scale** (`UIScale`, 50 to 100%): the health and stamina bars, equipment slots, soul counter and boss health bars drawn smaller, each toward its own corner or edge. `UIScaleWorld` does the same for the health bars and names over enemies and players and the lock-on marker.
- **Sharp on scaled displays** (`DpiAware`): with Windows scaling above 100% the game draws at the monitor's real pixels.
- **The mouse pointer hides** after 2 seconds without moving (`HideMouseCursor`).

## DXVK, HDR and G-SYNC / FreeSync

- **DXVK is on by default** (`DXVK`): the game's Direct3D 9 runs on Vulkan, which helps most where the draw thread is the limit (Blighttown at 4K: 96 FPS on Direct3D 9, 129 with DXVK).
- **HDR and variable refresh together** (`DxvkPresentThroughDxgi`): DXVK's frames go out through DXGI, so Windows HDR stays on with the same picture as Direct3D 9, and G-SYNC / FreeSync works in fullscreen and borderless alike.
- **Even frame pacing** (`DxvkPacedPresent`, `EvenFrameSteps`): each frame is shown a steady delay after the game made it, and while the game keeps to the cap every frame advances it by exactly the cap's step. With a 120 cap on a 240 Hz G-SYNC display, the refresh was as even as on Direct3D 9. Set `MaxFPS` to a rate your PC holds in busy areas, below your display's refresh rate.
- `DxvkHdrSwapChain` (`true` for scRGB, `hdr10`) sends frames to an HDR display in its own format; it made no difference to pacing in tests, so it is off.

## Faster, same picture

- **More of the draw thread's work done directly** (`FastEffects`, `FastRenderMath`): the effects library's particle curves and loops, the animated models' bone matrices, bounding boxes and square roots, each checked against the game's own code for the same results. Quelaag's Domain's heaviest view: 138.5 to 179.5 FPS; the Demon Ruins 120 to 167 FPS (same settings, 4K, RTX 3090).
- **Blighttown's unseen mirror is skipped** (`SkipUnusedReflection`): the game drew the whole scene a second time, small and mirrored, with nothing on screen showing it. About 4% more frames there.
- **Repeated render state calls skipped** too (`FilterSamplerStates`), and the game's write locks join its read locks without kernel calls (`FastReadLocks`).
- **Fewer loading hitches** (`PreloadTextures`): textures reach the graphics card while the game loads them, not mid-frame at their first draw.

## Fixes

- **Effects that act at intervals keep their rhythm** (`FixSpEffectTiming`): lava, heals and other effects that act every 0.1 or 0.2 s rounded each wait up to whole frames, so they acted at the wrong rate (a 60 HP/s effect gave 49 at 30 FPS, 55 at 60 and 58 at 240). Now 60 at any frame rate.
- **Bodies loading in no longer fling characters** (`FixVelocity`): the ragdoll's pull toward its pose is now the game's own per second, not per frame.
- **No more "Framerate is insufficient for Online play"** (`FixFramerateKick`): one pause of about 20 seconds (a stuck driver, a debugger, a phone app pausing the game) sent you to the title screen and kept the game offline until a restart.

## DSfix

DSfix by Durante is a legendary mod, and earlier versions of this mod expected it. This mod now takes its own approach to resolution, depth of field, effects and anti-aliasing, aimed at faithfulness, and some of what it does (16-bit colour, a depth of field measured against the original, effects at a matching resolution) could not be built on DSfix's. DSfix is still fully supported:

- This mod's picture settings take precedence by default. **`YieldToDSFix = true`** lets DSfix decide what it has its own settings for (resolution, anti-aliasing, SSAO, depth of field, texture filtering, HUD scale); the frame rate unlock, every fix, the speed-ups, the refresh rate, ultrawide and shadows stay this mod's.
- DSfix's resolution needs the game's own anti-aliasing off; with it on, the 3D picture fills only a corner of the screen (the log says so).
- If you play with DSfix, try this mod's own picture and tell us what you see. Details in the README's [Prepare to Die Edition and DSfix](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-FPS-Unlock-PTDE-Remastered#prepare-to-die-edition-and-dsfix).

## Steam Deck, Linux and phones

- **Steam Deck: keep the game's anti-aliasing off.** With it on, grass and other fine detail show black specks; not solved yet.
- **Phones** (Android with GameNative or Winlator): a `[Phones]` profile applies when the game runs translated (40 FPS cap, the game's own scene resolution and shadow map), the game no longer freezes for up to half a second while areas stream in (`CacheDriveType`), and `FastReadLocks` steps aside under translation.

**[Prepare to Co-op Edition](https://github.com/zofrea1/DS1-Prepare-to-Coop-PTDE) 1.9.0** includes FPS Unlock 2.3.0. If you have it, do not install this as well.

Details for every fix are in the README.

## Install

Copy the three files next to the game exe. Upgrading: replace `xinput1_3.dll` and `dxvk_d3d9.dll`; your INI keeps working, and new settings take their defaults when it does not list them (replace it too for the new layout and notes). Recommended: exclusive fullscreen with `FullscreenRefreshRate = 0`. Back up your saves. Steam Deck and Linux: launch option `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="xinput1_3=n,b" %command%`.

**Online play: no guarantees.** The mod modifies the game in memory; using it on official servers could risk a ban. Use at your own risk; no responsibility is accepted for bans or lost progress (see the README and LICENSE).

## Known limits

Riding the edge of a short step can leave you stuck against it at a high frame rate. In-engine cutscene cameras are blended; a hard cut still snaps, and pre-rendered movies stay 30 FPS. Send `PTDE-FPS-Unlock.log` and any `*-crash.dmp` with bug reports.
