# Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition, FPS Unlock v2.8.0

More of the world drawn around you, with the detailed pieces winning and no flicker, the objects of those rooms with them, and 4 GB of address space for the game. Files: `xinput1_3.dll`, `PTDE-FPS-Unlock.ini` and `dxvk_d3d9.dll`, next to `DARKSOULS.exe`. Current Steam build only (`SizeOfImage` `0x11C2000`).

## The game gets 4 GB of address space

- **`LargeAddressAware`** (new, on). `DARKSOULS.exe` is a 32-bit program without the "large address aware" flag, so Windows gives the game 2 GB of address space. At 4K with DXVK about 1.5 GB of that is in use on the Undead Burg's bridge alone, and more players in co-op, more of the world loaded or heavier settings can use up the rest: the game then crashes, often inside the graphics driver (a co-op host at 1.7 GB in use, a crash in NVIDIA's Vulkan driver). On its first start the mod sets the flag in `DARKSOULS.exe` (one byte; the original is kept as `DARKSOULS.exe.before-laa`), and from the next start the game has 4 GB: 2.8 GB free at the title screen instead of 0.8. Nothing is changed when the game already has 4 GB (the flag set before, or on the Steam Deck and Linux, where Proton gives it). Steam's "verify integrity of game files" puts the original back, and the next start sets the flag again. `false` leaves the exe as it is. The log's heartbeat now shows the address space left.

## Draw distance: `DrawMapPieces`

- **On by default: `adjacent`** (was `game`): the map pieces seen from the rooms next to yours are drawn too, so grass, rocks, ruins and torches no longer appear only once you reach a certain spot (the grass on the ledge below Firelink's overlook). Phones keep `game` (`PhoneDrawMapPieces`, in the phone profile). An INI from 2.7.0 lists `DrawMapPieces = game` and keeps it: set `adjacent` (or `adjacent+`) there to have it.

- **`adjacent+`** (new): the map pieces seen from the rooms up to three steps away, between `adjacent` and `all`: from the Undead Burg's bridge 33 of the map's 128 rooms (`adjacent` 10), with the street below the bridge's start, without the far parts of the map `all` brings.
- **The detailed piece wins.** Many surfaces exist twice: a detailed piece for the rooms that see it up close and a cheaper stand-in for the rooms that see it from afar (from the Undead Burg's bridge the game draws a lower Burg of 23,000 vertices standing in for 24 pieces of about 430,000). A stand-in is now hidden and the detailed pieces of its surfaces are drawn in its place, with the sun's shadows and the lights nearby that the stand-in leaves out. Where two rooms have their own versions of the same surfaces (a cliff built twice), the one your room shows stays. Where two versions overlap in one plane, the coarser is drawn a hair behind the finer; where they run apart and cross each other, as no depth offset keeps those apart, only one is drawn: the one your room shows, or of two added ones the finer. Every overlapping pair of map pieces in the game was measured for this from the maps' own data, not by hand. Nothing the game itself draws from your room is ever hidden. In 2.7.0 the cheaper piece won where both were drawn, and some surfaces flickered.
- **Coarse far backdrops only where the game draws them** (from Solaire's platform Darkroot's valley floor far below showed its cut edges as a dark slab).
- **A piece of another room that holds the player is left out**, as the game does from your room (at the bottom of Havel's tower Darkroot Basin's ground crossed the room at waist height). The pieces drawn in a stand-in's place are kept.
- **Each map's pieces by that map's own rooms** (a seam where the aqueduct meets the Undead Burg).
- **Their effects and lights come with them** (torch fire, mist, embers), and **`DrawMapObjects`** (new, on): their objects too, loaded as when you stand there: ladders, doors, crates, barrels, corpses with their items' glow (all 528 of the Undead Burg's stay where the map puts them; crates and barrels only move once broken).
- **Characters keep the game's rooms.** In 2.7.0 they were loaded and woken with the pieces, so with `all` the map's events could run early (Solaire went missing from the Undead Burg) and enemies far away were woken. Firelink Shrine's crow and its people stay loaded wherever Firelink is, updated every frame, checked to be safe (no event of theirs waits for them, and their talk sets nothing unless you are next to them).

Their cost where it is highest that we know of, on the Undead Burg's bridge looking down on the lower Burg, at 4K with everything else as shipped: `game` 307 FPS, `adjacent` 237, `adjacent+` 172, `all` 137 (render thread 3.2, 4.2, 5.8 and 7.3 ms a frame). At Firelink about 2% in any of them. On the Steam Deck, `adjacent+` averaged about 120 FPS playing through the Undead Burg. Address space is the same in all four.

## Occlusion culling

- **`OcclusionCulling`** (new, on): the pieces, map effects and objects `DrawMapPieces` adds are skipped while something in front hides them completely, checked every frame with the graphics card's occlusion queries (hidden once both its own drawing and its bounding box showed nothing, drawn again as soon as its box shows, and every eighth frame regardless). At the Demon Ruins bonfire with `all`, nearly all of Lost Izalith is behind walls: render thread 3.87 to 3.35 ms a frame; on the Undead Burg's bridge with `all` the added torch fire and mist, none of it visible there, 180 to 205 FPS; looking across the Undead Burg with its objects, 7.2 to 5.1 ms. The game's own pieces, characters, objects and shadows are never skipped, nor a model your room also draws. A piece the moving camera brings into view can appear a few frames late. `all`: every map piece, the game's own too (little gain, see the README).

## Faster

- **GPU** (at 4K, frozen views): the effects' upsample skips empty pixels, the scene is resolved without a copy, the depth of field is drawn straight into the scene, and depth copies write no colour nothing reads. Firelink 292.9 to 318.5 FPS (+8.7%), Anor Londo +13%, Blighttown +12%, Darkroot +18.5%, Quelaag's Domain +12%. Same picture.
- **The water's mirrored pass** (`SkipUnusedReflection`) is no longer drawn while everything that reads it is hidden: on the Undead Burg's bridge with `all` it was drawn every frame for water nobody saw (render thread 5.82 to 4.22 ms there, 171.6 to 236.3 FPS with everything above).
- **`FoliageSway`**: the wind's time once a frame instead of once per foliage draw.

## HUD and menus

- **The game's questions and plates follow the UI scale.** "Are you sure you want to quit the game?" and the other questions with OK and CANCEL now scale with `UIScaleMenus` about the middle of the screen (the mouse and the button guide with them), and the plates in the prompts' place ("Used Dungeon Cell Key") with `UIScale`, as the prompts do. They kept the game's size before.

## Fixed

- **`YieldToDSFix = true`** now also leaves `FixAspect`, `FixAspectUI` and `FullscreenRefreshRate` to DSfix, and `FixWater` steps back beside DSfix 3.0 (suggested by DDTM, DSfix 3.0's author).
- **`EffectLodDistance`** installs all four of its checks or none.
- **`FoliageSway`**: the wind's time no longer wraps once a frame.

## Install

Copy the three files next to the game exe. Upgrading from 2.7.0: replace `xinput1_3.dll`; your INI keeps working, and new settings take their defaults when it does not list them. The first start sets the large address aware flag in `DARKSOULS.exe` (see above); the game has 4 GB from the second start. Back up your saves. Steam Deck and Linux: launch option `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="xinput1_3=n,b" %command%`.

**Online play: no guarantees.** The mod modifies the game in memory; using it on official servers could risk a ban. Use at your own risk; no responsibility is accepted for bans or lost progress (see the README and LICENSE).
